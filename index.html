Kinly

Somebody in the family decides who gets through.

Kinly protects an older person from scam calls — without changing their phone, their number, or anything they have to remember. They are asked to do one thing, once: tap a link in a text and say yes.

Grandma should not have to decide whether an unknown caller is safe. Her family should decide.

What Kinly is not

a spam blocker a new phone number an app your mom has to learn an AI that decides for her a call recorder

Who is involved

Three people, and only one of them does any work

Everything about Kinly comes from one idea: the person doing the protecting and the person being protected are not the same person — and the person being protected should have to learn nothing at all.

Your parent can always say no — and always change their mind

Nothing starts until they agree. Once it has started, they can see who is looking after their calls, and they can end it themselves at any time by replying STOP to a text. That is not a setting buried in an app they will never open. It is one word, in the messages app they already use.

The heart of it

What happens when an unknown number rings

The order here matters more than anything else. The phone protects your parent immediately and on its own. Everything after that happens at your pace, and none of it can change what already happened on the phone.

The phone handles it instantly

Your parent’s phone already knows who is on the trusted list. It checks that list itself, in a fraction of a second, and acts. No waiting, no spinner, and no internet connection needed — it works in a basement, on a plane, or during an outage.

Happens on their phone

You get a notification

Someone new tried to reach your parent. You see it when you next pick up your phone. Your parent is not asked anything and is not interrupted.

Happens on your phone

You can ask the caller who they are

Kinly sends them a short text asking for their name and their reason. You are shown the exact words before it goes, every time. You cannot write your own message — and neither can anyone else who ever gets into your account.

Your choice, never automatic

They reply in their own words

You read exactly what they wrote, unedited. A real dentist’s office answers plainly. A scammer usually does not answer at all.

Comes back to you, not to your parent

You decide

Let them through, block them, or leave it. There is no deadline and no pressure, and nothing gets decided for you if you do nothing.

Always a person, never the software

Their phone updates within seconds

The decision reaches your parent’s phone almost immediately, and the phone keeps working from it even if it later loses signal entirely.

Back to their phone

One thing to know about iPhones

This full loop works on Android phones. On an iPhone, Apple does not let any app see that a call came in — so Kinly can still block and label numbers, but it cannot tell you about a call your parent did not answer. That is Apple’s rule, not our choice, and we say so during setup rather than letting a family find out the hard way.

The default for a stranger

Every message we send

This is all of it — there are no others

Kinly can send exactly three text messages, and you are reading all three. We never pretend to be a bank, a phone company, the government, or your parent.

1 — to your parent, at the very start
Kinly: Sarah would like to help protect calls to this number. Review and approve or decline: kinly.app/a/7Kq2… This link expires in 15 minutes. If you weren't expecting this, ignore this message. Reply STOP to opt out.

This is the moment your parent agrees. It names who is asking, says plainly what will happen, and tells them to ignore it if they weren’t expecting it — because a text nobody was expecting is exactly how scams start.

2 — to a caller, only when you choose to ask
You tried to reach someone who uses Kinly call protection. Please reply with your name and why you're calling. Example: Sarah from Dr. Smith's office - calling about an appointment. Reply STOP to opt out.

Notice what it does not say: not who they called, not your parent’s name, not your name, not even that somebody is reviewing them. It asks a question and gives away nothing.

Kinly summarized what this caller said. It can’t verify whether any of it is true.

The caller’s own words always come first, unedited. The summary sits underneath, clearly labeled as a claim rather than a fact. There is no score, no “probably safe”, no traffic light — because the moment a family starts trusting a score, they have stopped reading, and a good scammer writes a very convincing message.

3 — to someone you decide to let through
Kinly: You can now call this number.

There is deliberately no message for blocking. Telling a scammer they were caught just teaches them to try a new number, and telling a real person they were blocked invites an awkward phone call to your parent.

The rules we built it on

Six promises the design has to keep

If anything breaks, the call comes through

When the phone cannot read its own list for any reason, it lets the call ring and tells you something is wrong.

A scam call getting through is fixable. A silenced ambulance is not.

Nobody can read your family’s numbers — including us

Phone numbers are scrambled before they are ever stored, using two separate keys kept in two separate places.

If our entire database were stolen tomorrow, it would contain no readable numbers.

It works with no signal

Your parent’s phone never has to ask us anything to protect them. It already knows the answer and decides by itself.

Protection that needs a good connection is not protection.

The AI never gets a vote

It summarizes what a caller claimed and stops there. There is nowhere in the entire system to store a verdict, so no screen could ever show you one.

You decide who may call your mother. That is the whole point.

A stranger can never change anything

The most any caller can ever do is leave a reply for you to read. No message from outside can add itself to a list or take itself off one.

Otherwise the people you're protecting against would hold the controls.

Your parent keeps the final say

They approve before anything starts, they can see who is managing their calls, and they can end it themselves at any time.

Protecting someone is not the same as taking their phone away.

Your mother should not have to become suspicious of her own phone.

She keeps her number, her handset, and her habits. Somebody who loves her quietly decides who gets through.
